Research

We model how extreme events ripple through infrastructure and communities, and design decisions that make both more resilient.

Natural disasters, cyberattacks and pandemics keep revealing how vulnerable our communities and built environment are. We combine advanced AI, statistical learning and robust optimization to examine the impacts of chronic and acute shocks on sociotechnical systems, develop risk-informed decision models, and identify cost-effective adaptation measures.

New · NSF FIRE program · $2 million

REKINDLE: preparing communities for wildfire's cascading disasters

Wildfires can set off debris flows and flash floods that cut roads and interrupt power and water for communities already struggling to recover. REKINDLE (Resilient Engineering through Knowledge Integration for NetworkeD Lifelines and Ecosystems) will develop AI-powered tools that connect these hazards, the infrastructure they disrupt and the people affected, so decision-makers can plan for them together.

Hazard risks

GeoAI and physics-based models of wildfire and the debris flows and flash floods that follow, with uncertainty bounds.

Module 2

Infrastructure risks

Graph machine learning and network science for cascading failures across power, water and transportation.

Module 3

Societal risks

Household surveys, focus groups and serious games to measure the social burden of lifeline disruptions.

Module 4

Resilience decisions

Multi-level optimization, scaled with AI, for pre-fire hardening and post-fire recovery.

U.S. DOE Office of Electricity · 2025

American-Made Science Synthesis Prize: AI for a disaster-resilient, renewable grid

The Science Synthesis Prize, launched in May 2024 as part of DOE's Grid Resource Integration Technology (GRIT) prize series, asked teams to map the state of renewable energy integration and identify the key barriers and investment opportunities. Team OASIS UB was one of eight winners, each awarded $10,000.

DOE winners announcement

Team
OASIS UB, led by Dr. Sayanti Mukherjee
Winners announced
September 4, 2025
Focus
Intermittency, supply-demand mismatch, cybersecurity and natural hazards in decentralized grids, and where AI can help
In the media
Niagara Gazette, Nov 2025

What the synthesis covers

A systematic literature review of solar, wind, geothermal and hydrogen integration: current U.S. integration practices, the main barriers (intermittency, integration cost, storage, forecasting and aging grid resilience), the opportunities that address them, and policy recommendations grounded in those opportunities.

The poster extends this to decentralized grids, matching AI methods such as deep forecasting, reinforcement learning, anomaly detection and spatiotemporal models to each challenge, with real case studies like the Texas 2021 freeze and California microgrids.

Research areas

Infrastructure

Critical infrastructure risk and resilience

Lifelines such as power, gas, water and transportation depend on each other, yet topology and interdependency data are often missing. We use simulation, Bayesian networks, generative AI and predictive analytics to assess vulnerability under incomplete information.

  • Vulnerability of interdependent systems under incomplete information
  • Sewer pipe failure risk and its key drivers
Energy

Energy systems resilience

Extreme heat, cold, storms and drought threaten the energy system. We use statistical learning, GeoAI, remote sensing and deep learning to quantify risks and support planning and policy.

  • Climate-energy nexus under deep uncertainty
  • Natural disaster impacts on energy systems
  • Resilience investment decisions for grid regulators
  • Renewable integration challenges and policy
Hazards

Impacts of extreme events

Wildfires, heat waves, storms and flooding affect health, infrastructure and communities. We use GeoAI, remote sensing, interpretable ML and econometrics to design interventions.

  • Drivers of wildfire spread
  • Wildfire-induced failure risk of interconnected infrastructure
  • Minimizing wildfire-induced power outage risk
  • Disaster impacts on economic growth
Health

Population health and healthcare analytics

Data-centric models for mortality and morbidity risk, disease spread and mental health, informing health policy and resource allocation.

  • Health behaviors and adolescent suicide attempts
  • Community health and built environment nexus
  • Suicide disparities across metropolitan areas
Justice

Community safety and social justice

Interpretable AI and optimization to understand the geographic and socioeconomic context of crime and support fair allocation of public safety resources.

  • Socio-environmental factors in felony and misdemeanor rates
  • Spatiotemporal attack patterns of terrorist organizations
  • Crime risk for women in developing countries
Mobility

Human mobility data analytics

GPS, phone, social media and transit data reveal how people move before, during and after disasters, and who loses access to critical services.

  • Income segregation in activity spaces under disaster
  • Forecasting shelter demand
  • Two-stage forecasting of service demand
  • Equitable access to food stores under disasters
